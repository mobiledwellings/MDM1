TOP NOTE: This article is about Dennis and Marty and their bus, Levon Short Notice, which we toured at Bus Fair 2026. This article was written by Justin in the style of the extremely talented and entertaining [Bill Bryson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Bryson) with a head-start from Claude.

[[VIDEO 1]]

On July 19th, 1987, the Grateful Dead played Autzen Stadium in Eugene, Oregon, and Dennis was there. Among Deadheads, your first show is the day you "got on the bus," and that is how he still tells it: "I jumped on the bus on July 19th, 1987." But he also saw an actual bus that day, a big painted one, and he decided on the spot that he had to have one.

Most of us pick up a souvenir at concert, a t-shirt perhaps. But Dennis took home with him a new lifelong passion that eventually lead him to build a home in a school bus, 37 years later. 

![A young man in a hat crouching on the roof of a Volkswagen Beetle painted with a Grateful Dead steal your face skull, hills behind him.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/grateful-dead-beetle.jpg)
caption: From the family album: a painted Beetle, some years before the bus.

Dennis and Marty's bus is called Levon Short Notice (say it out loud), and it is, as I said into a camera at the end of the tour and will happily say again in print, the most Grateful Dead bus I have ever seen.

![LEVON SHORT NOTICE painted in big black letters across the rear of the bus, above a cargo rack and a Washington license plate reading GET ON.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/levon-short-notice-bumper.jpg)
caption: Say it out loud.

## Twelve years of airports

Dennis and Marty live in the Columbia River Gorge, and for twelve years they have flown around the country to see live music. Anyone who has done this knows the arithmetic: the show lasts three hours and getting to it takes two days, most of them spent removing your shoes for the TSA.

![Dennis and Marty sitting in camp chairs beside the marbles board in front of their bus, which is painted with Bigfoot versions of the Grateful Dead dancing bears.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/dennis-and-marty-bigfoot-mural.jpg)
caption: Dennis and Marty, in front of the Bigfoot bears.

"We're getting to the age where we're hoping that we can start driving to some of those places," they say, "and just be a little more comfortable along the way." The ideal, as they describe it, is a vehicle that works like a voice assistant: "Hey Siri, I want to go to Jazz Fest, go, and then we just go."

![Dennis and Marty walking hand in hand down the street in front of their painted bus.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/dennis-and-marty-hand-in-hand.jpg)
caption: Dennis and Marty, going.

## A bus that was already half done

They bought it a couple of years ago, and it came school bus yellow. The previous owner had done perhaps 55% of the inside: two banks of cabinets, live edge countertops and a wood ceiling. Dennis and Marty gutted a good deal of the rest and rebuilt it as their own. If you are standing at the start of the same project, [we answer questions about how to pull it off at Skoolie Support](/skoolie-support).

![A plain yellow 40-foot school bus parked on grass beneath a dramatic sky, before the paint job.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/yellow-thomas-before.jpg)
caption: The Thomas in its original livery, about to have a very big year.

The ceiling, however, was not up for discussion. "I told Dennis, absolutely, we are not getting rid of the wood ceiling," Marty says. "I said, do whatever you want anywhere else, but this has to stay." This is, I think, the whole secret of a successful marriage, expressed in terms of interior joinery.

![The interior of the bus looking forward, with a pale wood plank ceiling, a skylight, and orange cushioned benches below.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/wood-ceiling-skylight.jpg)
caption: The ceiling that stayed. "The color just feels so natural," Marty says.

## A muralist from a rest area

The outside, meanwhile, was still yellow, and they wanted something that said who they were. Then, in a sentence that I have now read several times without it becoming any less surprising: "We found a girl at a rest area."

Most of us meet people at rest areas only in the sense of standing behind them for the hand dryer. Dennis and Marty met Lilith Lavender, a traveling muralist who works as [@lilithmakesart](https://www.instagram.com/lilithmakesart/). They made friends quickly, and she flew in from Arizona and painted the whole bus.

![The bus primed plain white and parked on gravel under a big blue sky with clouds.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/primed-white.jpg)
caption: Yellow no more: the blank canvas.

![The white bus partway through painting, with a yellow and green lightning bolt down its rear corner.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/lightning-bolt-in-progress.jpg)
caption: The first lightning bolt goes on.

Her brief was generous. "We gave Lily, our artist, our friend, our sister, full license to do whatever she wanted," they say, "with just a couple of caveats, but not too much."

![Handwritten lettering low on the side of the bus reading artwork by @lilithmakesart.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/artwork-by-lilithmakesart.jpg)
caption: Signed, like any good painting.

## A walk around the outside

The mural is best taken as a slow lap of the bus, and it rewards one. Down one side a steam locomotive comes rolling down the tracks toward Mount Hood, which stands in for Oregon and Washington.

![A painted red steam locomotive on the side of the bus, heading toward a yellow mountain set against an orange sky.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/steam-locomotive-mount-hood-mural.jpg)

Next come the bears. They wanted the Grateful Dead dancing bears, and they also wanted Bigfoot, a dilemma Lilith settled by painting Bigfoot dancing in the bears' famous pose. There are three of the five. "I don't think it would fit all five of the bears," they say, "but we're happy with that."

![Three Bigfoot figures painted in the pose of the Grateful Dead dancing bears along the side of the bus, beside its open door.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/bigfoot-dancing-bears-mural.jpg)
caption: The Grateful Dead dancing bears, as reimagined by the Pacific Northwest.

After that comes a Jerry moon over a tumbledown shack.

![A crescent moon and stars painted over dark pine trees beside the door of the bus.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/jerry-moon-mural.jpg)

The other side is the one that means the most to them. One of their first big trips together was to see the Fare Thee Well shows, the band's 50th anniversary, and while they were in San Francisco they had their picture taken in front of 710 Ashbury, the house where the Grateful Dead lived in the sixties. They have done the same on every trip back since. So that side has the Painted Ladies, with Uncle Sam leaping out at you.

![The Painted Ladies Victorian houses of San Francisco painted along the side of the bus, with a skeleton Uncle Sam leaping out of the scene.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/painted-ladies-uncle-sam-mural.jpg)
caption: San Francisco, with Uncle Sam leaping out at you.

From there the mural runs up through the Haight, across the Golden Gate Bridge and home to Oregon, where a terrapin catches a wave on a windsurfing board. An ordinary terrapin might simply surf, but Dennis and Marty are from the Gorge, which is to windsurfing roughly what Nashville is to country music, "so we had to put a sail on it to make it make sense for where we live."

![A painted terrapin riding a windsurfing board on a breaking wave, with a sail behind it.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/windsurfing-terrapin-mural.jpg)
caption: A terrapin, adapted for local conditions.

Lilith also painted a lyric along the way: RAMBLE ON BABY down one flank and SETTLE DOWN EASY on the other, both from "Ramble On Rose." For a couple whose plan is to leave on short notice and go anywhere, it is a fairly complete set of instructions.

![RAMBLE ON BABY painted in red bubble letters on a white cloud along the side of the bus, beside a lightning bolt.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/ramble-on-baby-mural.jpg)

![SETTLE DOWN EASY painted in gold letters across a purple panel on the side of the bus.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/settle-down-easy-mural.jpg)

## Get on

The front of the bus says ANYWHERE across the top, which is their answer to the question of where they are going. The license plate says GET ON. It is a short sentence, but in Deadhead terms it is more or less a complete philosophy.

![The front bumper of the bus with a license plate reading GET ON, framed by painted stars.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/get-on-license-plate.jpg)
caption: GET ON, short for get on the bus.

Behind the windshield sits Bobby Bones, a skeleton in tie-dye who serves as stand-in driver whenever the bus is parked. "He keeps all the rubber on the road." 

![A skeleton in a tie-dye shirt and a yellow hat sitting at the wheel in the driver's seat of the bus.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/bobby-bones-driver.jpg)
caption: Bobby Bones, on duty.

On the roof is a flagpole of about eighteen feet, putting the top of the flag something like 27 feet off the ground. Dennis came up with this, Marty explains, and its purpose is practical: "It helps us find our own house when we stumble out of the festival." Any engineer will tell you the best designs come from understanding how the product will really be used.

![The painted bus parked in a festival field with tall pines behind it, its flags flying high above the roof.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/bus-and-flagpole.jpg)
caption: Home, as seen from the far end of the festival.

## Everything is moving

The first thing you notice inside is that a great deal of it hangs from something, and when the bus is moving all of it is moving too. "We haven't had any problems," they say. Their approach to decoration is admirably straightforward: "Whatever we like, we throw it on this bus." This is how a bus ends up with a small garden built entirely out of Lego.

![The view through the windshield from behind the driver's seat, with mushroom garlands, a disco ball, a dreamcatcher and a Be Nice to the Bus Driver sign hanging over a dash lined with figurines and plants.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/view-from-the-drivers-seat.jpg)
caption: The view from the driver's seat. All of it moves.

The household also includes Stella, a fairly old and very lovely boxer, who was staying with Uncle Robert the day we filmed but whose bed has a spot of its own.

![Dennis taking a selfie cheek to cheek with Stella, an old boxer with her tongue out.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/dennis-and-stella.jpg)
caption: Dennis and Stella.

## The best seat in the house

There was, at first, nowhere for Marty to sit while the bus was moving. "Marty didn't have a place to sit," Dennis says. A friend spotted a captain's chair on Marketplace, they had to have it, and it now sits behind the driver with its own seatbelt and armrests, bolted to the wheel well.

He is clearly proud of how well anchored it is. "I might go out the window in the driver's seat, but she's going to stay right there" which feels like a slightly romantic thing to say.  

![A cream leather captain's chair bolted into the bus behind the driver's seat, beside a window.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/martys-captains-chair.jpg)
caption: Marty's chair, which is going nowhere.

## The marbles championship

Further back are the kitchen and a dinette with a live edge table, which turns out to be the emotional center of the bus. "Even on a cozy night when we're at home and the bus is parked in the driveway," Marty says, "I will often say, let's go out on the bus and play marbles."

There is a competitive edge to this. "My mission is to become the new marbles champion," she says, "because Dennis thinks he's the champion." But Dennis regards the matter as settled. We're not sure who will win this battle long term. 

![Dennis and Marty at the live edge dinette, mid-game on a wooden marbles board.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/marbles-at-the-dinette.jpg)
caption: The championship, ongoing.

They do not cook much inside; most of that happens on their trailer outside. The counter is for prepping food, laying it out, and, they claim, making "the best sandwiches that you're ever going to have." Having seen the evidence, I am not prepared to argue.

![Marty assembling sandwiches on the live edge counter, with sliced avocado, bread and condiments laid out.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/making-sandwiches.jpg)
caption: Exhibit A, in production.

There are seven skylights, a figure they follow at once with the two words every bus owner would like on their headstone: "No leaks." At night there are touch lights, run from small solar panels on the roof, "so when we're having game night or splitting a bottle of wine or whatever, we do have some nice soft light."

## Mr. Bubble

At the back, the old emergency exit has been relabeled DRAMATIC EXIT, and you pass under it into what they call the boudoir.

![A red sign reading DRAMATIC EXIT over the rear door of the bus, framed by tie-dye curtains and a dreamcatcher.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/dramatic-exit.jpg)

The bathroom, they admit, "gets a lot of laughs sometimes." There is not a great deal of privacy, so "we have to work together if you want some privacy." What there is, however, is a clawfoot bathtub from 1952, painted by a well-known Harley-Davidson tank painter "whose name escapes me always." It is one of the crown jewels of the bus, and it gets a lot of attention.

![A 1952 clawfoot bathtub painted in deep turquoise with a gold rim, set inside the bus.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/clawfoot-tub.jpg)
caption: The 1952 clawfoot tub, painted by a Harley-Davidson tank painter.

![Dennis in the bath grinning at the camera, his beard covered in bubbles.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/mr-bubble.jpg)
caption: "Me and Mr. Bubble."

The sign on the wall above it reads, with some justice, "Take a bath you dirty hippie."

![A sign reading Take a Bath You Dirty Hippie on the wall of the bus, beside a carved wooden mask.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/dirty-hippie-sign.jpg)

Nearby is a drum kit, and a supply of shakers and drumsticks, which brings us to the bus's one real shortcoming as a festival vehicle. "Neither one of us are musicians," they say, "but we have so much love in our heart for live music." So the instruments are bait. Any musician who wanders aboard is encouraged to serenade them. Now THAT is definitely romantic!

![An electronic drum kit with a gold stool, beside a basket that says grateful.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/drum-kit.jpg)
caption: Bait, for passing musicians.

## The bedroom

Then come his and hers closets ("or mostly hers") and the bedroom, where the bed has delivered, by one account, "the best night sleep that I've had in probably 10 years."

![The bed at the back of the bus seen through a beaded curtain, with a Stay Cozy pillow, a striped blanket and a disco ball overhead.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/stay-cozy-bed.jpg)
caption: Stay cozy, as instructed.

Over it hangs a neon sign that reads THE MUSIC SOUNDS BETTER WITH YOU, a recent addition. Marty mentioned earlier that Dennis likes TVs everywhere. There is another one here.

![A blue neon sign on the ceiling of the bus reading The Music Sounds Better With You.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/music-sounds-better-neon.jpg)

## More solar

Asked what they would tell their past selves, the answer came back at once, and it was about power: "encourage more solar, less other things as far as power goes." We hear it on a great many tours, and I have yet to meet anyone who wished they had less.

![A drone view looking down on the bus's long white roof, with two small solar panels among the vents and skylights and a blue canopy beside it.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/drone-roof-solar.jpg)
caption: The entire rooftop solar array: two small panels on 40 feet of roof.

## What they bring from home

Its first proper trip was last summer, and by their count maybe 500 people have climbed aboard since. They love showing it. "It's what we bring from home and our heart," they say, "and we just display it for everybody to see."

![A crowd of friends packed into the kitchen and dinette of the bus, laughing and talking.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/bus-full-of-people.jpg)
caption: A quiet night in, by their standards.

The bus has also changed them, though not in the direction you might expect from a vehicle designed for leaving. "This entire year has been about reconnecting," they say. "So building community? That has been really kind of my focus for myself and for our little family. I really feel like it's helped keep me grounded, but also opened me up to a lot of stuff."

![The painted bus parked on a roadside pullout with the snow-covered peak of Mount Hood rising behind it.](/blog/grateful-dead-bus/levon-under-mount-hood.jpg)
caption: Levon Short Notice, under the real Mount Hood.

Dennis got on the bus in Eugene in 1987. It just took a while for the bus to arrive.
